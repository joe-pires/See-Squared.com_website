iHow This Guide Was Built

The Bureau of Automotive Repair (BAR) does not publish real past Smog Check Technician exam questions, so no honest practice test — this one included — can claim to reproduce actual exam items word‑for‑word. What follows is a custom‑built 50‑question practice exam, written from scratch and modeled directly on BAR's official exam content outline, its topic weighting, and the Smog Check Manual / Smog Check Reference Guide, plus current program regulations from bar.ca.gov.

The real BAR Repair Technician exam runs 120 questions over 3 hours across three content areas: Discharging Obligations to Consumers (19%), Diagnosing Test Failures (67%), and Performing Repairs (14%). This guide's 50 questions are weighted the same way and ordered easiest to hardest, so it works as a genuine warm‑up test rather than a topic list.

Two things make it more current than a lot of study material in circulation: it's written entirely around OBD‑based testing for 2000‑and‑newer vehicles, and it includes BAR's October 1, 2025 rule change requiring all supported OBD readiness monitors to be set — eliminating the older model‑year‑based “one monitor can stay unset” exemptions most pre‑2025 guides still describe. Questions 16, 35, and 42 are built directly around that change.

This is a practice tool, not a substitute for BAR's own materials. Verify current dollar figures, income thresholds, and regulation dates directly at bar.ca.gov before the real exam — program figures like Consumer Assistance Program caps are adjusted periodically.

Easy — Foundational Knowledge Q1–15

Q1 Easy Consumer Obligations / Program Rules

Under California's Consolidated Smog Check Program, which type of station is licensed to certify a “directed” vehicle or a gross polluter referred through the STAR program?

Why

Ordinary (non-STAR) Test-and-Repair stations can inspect and repair most vehicles, but California limits who may certify the higher-risk cases — directed vehicles and gross polluters — to STAR-certified stations or a BAR Referee facility. This tiering exists because STAR stations must meet stricter accuracy, equipment, and performance standards, giving the state more confidence in the result on vehicles most likely to be problem cases.

Q2 Easy Diagnosing Test Failures

For a 2000-and-newer gasoline vehicle, California's Smog Check uses which primary test method in place of a tailpipe dynamometer test?

Why

Since 2013, California has used the OBD Inspection System (OIS) as the primary test for 2000-and-newer OBD II vehicles: the analyzer connects to the 16-pin DLC and checks MIL commanded status, stored/pending codes, and monitor readiness instead of measuring tailpipe gases on a dynamometer. Tailpipe/dyno testing (BAR-97, ASM, TSI) is reserved for pre-2000 vehicles and specific program-area/vehicle-type exceptions.

Q3 Easy Consumer Obligations / Program Rules

Per SAE J1962, where must the OBD-II Data Link Connector (DLC) be located and how accessible must it be?

Why

SAE J1962 requires the 16-pin trapezoidal DLC to be within reach of the driver in the normal seated position, most commonly under the dash near the steering column, and reachable without tools. See Diagram 1. A missing, damaged, relocated outside spec, or physically blocked DLC is an automatic OBD inspection failure — this comes up constantly with aftermarket steering wheel/pedal swaps, kill switches, and audio installs that reroute wiring across that spot.

Q4 Easy Diagnosing Test Failures

On a modern OBD-II vehicle, a steadily illuminated Malfunction Indicator Lamp (MIL) / check engine light at the time of inspection results in:

Why

A commanded-on MIL means the PCM itself has determined there is an emissions-related fault serious enough to warrant the driver's attention. Because the entire OIS test is built around trusting the vehicle's own onboard diagnostics, a lit MIL is an automatic fail, independent of drivability or any external tailpipe reading.

Q5 Easy Diagnosing Test Failures

What is a “pending” diagnostic trouble code?

Why

Many faults use “two-trip” logic: the first time a fault is seen it is stored as pending; if it recurs on a subsequent drive cycle under similar conditions it becomes a confirmed/stored code and the MIL is commanded on. A pending code is useful diagnostic information even though it hasn't (yet) failed the vehicle on its own.

Q6 Easy Diagnosing Test Failures

What is the basic function of a three-way catalytic converter?

Why

“Three-way” refers to the three pollutants it treats at once: it oxidizes carbon monoxide (CO) and unburned hydrocarbons (HC) into CO2 and water, while reducing oxides of nitrogen (NOx) back into N2 and O2. It relies on the engine running in a tight closed-loop band around stoichiometric (14.7:1) to do all three reactions efficiently at the same time.

Q7 Easy Diagnosing Test Failures

What is the primary purpose of the Evaporative Emission (EVAP) control system?

Why

Gasoline vapor is a significant source of reactive hydrocarbon emissions if simply vented. The EVAP system (see Diagram 2) seals the fuel tank, captures vapor in an activated-charcoal canister, and periodically purges it into the intake under PCM control so it's burned in the cylinders rather than released to the air.

Q8 Easy Consumer Obligations / Program Rules

Which vehicles are currently exempt from both the biennial and change-of-ownership Smog Check requirements in California?

Why

New gasoline vehicles are exempt from Smog Check for their first several years (currently model years ≤ 4 years old) under the assumption that a nearly new vehicle's emissions systems are still functioning as designed. Vehicles aged 5–8 model years generally only need a change-of-ownership test, while vehicles 9+ model years old need both a biennial test and a change-of-ownership test.

Q9 Easy Diagnosing Test Failures

A generic OBD-II code beginning with “P0” (for example, P0301) is:

Why

SAE standardized the generic P0xxx code set so a P0301 (cylinder 1 misfire) means the same thing whether you're scanning a Ford, Toyota, or GM. Manufacturer-specific codes use the P1xxx (and B/C/U equivalents in their proprietary ranges) format and can vary in meaning between OEMs, but the core P0/P2/P3 generic sets are common across the industry — which is exactly what lets a $30 generic scanner read a MIL-on code on almost any car built since 1996.

Q10 Easy Diagnosing Test Failures

What does an upstream oxygen (or air-fuel ratio) sensor primarily allow the PCM to do?

Why

The upstream sensor is the PCM's real-time feedback for fuel control (see Diagram 5). It reports the actual oxygen content left in the exhaust, and the PCM trims injector pulse width up or down to chase the target mixture — this is the fuel trim loop that both drives day-to-day drivability and underlies several of the smog-relevant diagnostic codes on the exam.

Q11 Easy Consumer Obligations / Program Rules

A vehicle fails Smog Check and the owner cannot get it to pass even after a documented minimum expenditure on emissions-related repairs. What relief may they be eligible for?

Why

If a vehicle still won't pass after the owner has spent at least the required minimum on qualifying emissions repairs (verified by invoices, generally arranged through a Referee visit), a repair cost waiver lets it register for one cycle without passing. This dollar threshold is set by BAR and periodically adjusted, so a technician should confirm the current figure at bar.ca.gov rather than quoting an old number from memory.

Q12 Easy Diagnosing Test Failures

Which condition would most directly cause a P0171 (System Too Lean, Bank 1) code on a modern port- or direct-injected engine?

Why

P0171 means the PCM has run out of positive fuel trim correction trying to compensate for a mixture that's leaner than commanded. Since the trim can only add fuel, the root cause is almost always unmetered air getting in (vacuum/intake leak) or not enough fuel getting delivered (weak pump, clogged filter/injector, low fuel pressure).

Q13 Easy Diagnosing Test Failures

What does a positive long-term fuel trim (LTFT) value indicate the PCM is doing?

Why

Fuel trim is a correction factor, not a raw measurement. Positive trim means the PCM is commanding more fuel than its baseline map to correct a mixture it's reading as too lean; negative trim means it's pulling fuel to correct a mixture reading too rich. See Diagram 5 for the full feedback loop.

Q14 Easy Consumer Obligations / Program Rules

What is the purpose of the BAR Referee program?

Why

Referees, based at community college campuses statewide, exist for the edge cases: engine swaps, kit cars/specially constructed vehicles, out-of-state vehicles missing emissions labels, and re-inspections when a station's result is disputed. They perform a more thorough visual, functional, and (when applicable) tailpipe evaluation than a routine test-and-repair visit.

Q15 Easy Diagnosing Test Failures

What is the function of the Positive Crankcase Ventilation (PCV) system?

Why

Combustion blow-by past the piston rings pressurizes the crankcase with unburned fuel, combustion byproducts, and oil vapor. The PCV valve/system meters that gas back into the intake charge so it's combusted rather than vented, which is both an emissions-control measure and part of normal engine ventilation — a stuck or disconnected PCV can create a vacuum leak large enough to trigger lean codes.

Medium — Applied Diagnosis & Current Regulations Q16–35

Q16 Medium Consumer Obligations / Program Rules

Effective October 1, 2025, BAR updated its OBD readiness monitor rule. Under the current rule, what is required to pass the OBD portion of a Smog Check?

Why

This is a genuinely current fact worth knowing cold: before October 2025, BAR allowed specific monitors to stay unset by model year and fuel type (e.g., one unset monitor on 1996–99 gas vehicles, an unset EVAP monitor on 2000+ gas vehicles, unset DPF/NMHC catalyst monitors on 2007+ diesels). BAR eliminated those blanket exemptions because they let vehicles with a genuinely malfunctioning emissions system still pass. If a study guide printed before late 2025 says an unset EVAP monitor is acceptable on a 2000-and-newer car, that guide is now out of date.

Q17 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

A technician scans a 2019 turbocharged direct-injection sedan and finds freeze-frame data attached to a stored P0300 code. What is the primary diagnostic value of that freeze-frame data?

Why

Freeze frame is a single frozen snapshot, not a data log. Its value is that it tells you the operating conditions present the instant the fault matured — whether the misfire happened at idle, under load, cold, or hot — so you can reproduce those conditions on a test drive instead of guessing.

Q18 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

Why is intake-valve carbon buildup a more common complaint on direct-injection (GDI) engines than on port-injected engines?

Why

Port fuel injection sprays fuel (with its detergent package) directly onto the back of the intake valve, which has a natural cleaning effect. GDI injects straight into the cylinder, so the valve's back side only ever sees air — plus whatever oil vapor comes through the PCV and exhaust gas from the EGR. Over time that residue bakes into hard carbon, which is why walnut-shell media blasting or chemical intake cleaning has become a routine GDI maintenance/repair item.

Q19 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

A 2017 turbocharged 1.5L engine sets P0171 and P2187 (system too lean at idle) shortly after an intake system repair. Which fuel-trim pattern most strongly points to a vacuum/boost-side leak downstream of the mass air flow (MAF) sensor as the cause?

Why

An unmetered air leak admits a roughly fixed amount of extra air. At idle, total airflow is very low, so that fixed leak represents a large percentage of the total charge — a big lean shift. As RPM and load climb, the same leak becomes a smaller fraction of a much larger total airflow, so the lean shift shrinks. That idle-worse, cruise-better trim signature is the classic fingerprint of a leak on the fresh-air side after the MAF (versus a fuel-delivery problem, which tends to show up more evenly, or worse, under load).

Q20 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

What does a P0420 (Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold, Bank 1) code fundamentally indicate the PCM has detected?

Why

A healthy catalyst stores and releases oxygen as it works, which smooths out the sharp rich/lean switching seen upstream into a comparatively flat downstream signal (Diagram 3). As the catalyst's washcoat degrades, that oxygen-storage buffering capacity drops, and the downstream sensor starts switching in a pattern that increasingly resembles the upstream sensor. The PCM's catalyst monitor compares switching frequency/amplitude between the two sensors and fails when they become too similar — it's an indirect efficiency inference, not a direct temperature or flow measurement.

Upstream B1S1 — fast switching, rich/lean Downstream B1S2 — flat, near-steady (cat is buffering O2)

A degraded catalyst loses oxygen-storage capacity, so B1S2 starts mimicking B1S1's rapid switching — the PCM compares switch-ratio/amplitude of both to decide pass/fail (a P0420/P0430-type monitor).

Q21 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

Before condemning a catalytic converter for a P0420, which diagnostic step should be performed first?

Why

A perfectly good catalyst can trip P0420 because something upstream is feeding it a bad signal or genuinely damaging it: an exhaust leak near the downstream sensor draws in outside air and skews the reading; a sluggish upstream sensor or a persistent lean/rich condition changes what the converter actually has to do; and unresolved misfires dump raw fuel into the converter and cook it prematurely. Skipping this step is the single most common reason a “repeat” P0420 comes back after an unnecessary converter replacement.

Q22 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

A P0401 (EGR Insufficient Flow Detected) code on a 2018 direct-injection 4-cylinder is most commonly traced to:

Why

See Diagram 4: EGR passages carry hot, sooty exhaust gas, and over time carbon narrows or completely plugs the valve orifice, the intake-side passage, or the small sense ports feeding a DPFE (differential pressure) sensor. Because the PCM is commanding flow it isn't actually getting, it sets P0401 for insufficient flow. This is one of the most common GDI-era EGR complaints precisely because these engines already run more carbon-prone induction systems.

Simulate:

EGR valve fully closed — no exhaust gas is recirculated at idle or cold start.

P0401 (insufficient flow): passages/valve/DPFE ports clogged with carbon — common on GDI engines. P0402 (excessive flow): valve stuck open, causing a rough or stalling idle.

Q23 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

What symptom would you expect from a P0402 (EGR Excessive Flow Detected) code, as opposed to P0401?

Why

The EGR valve is supposed to be fully closed at idle and at wide-open throttle. If it's stuck partially open or leaking internally, inert exhaust gas dilutes the idle mixture at exactly the moment the engine can least tolerate it, producing a rough, hunting, or stalling idle — the opposite drivability signature from P0401's typically NOx-related, less-obviously-felt insufficient-flow condition.

Simulate:

EGR valve fully closed — no exhaust gas is recirculated at idle or cold start.

P0401 (insufficient flow): passages/valve/DPFE ports clogged with carbon — common on GDI engines. P0402 (excessive flow): valve stuck open, causing a rough or stalling idle.

Q24 Medium Performing Repairs

On a vehicle equipped with cylinder deactivation, why must a technician confirm the system is fully re-enabled and functioning normally before running readiness monitors after a repair?

Why

Deactivating cylinders changes exhaust volume, temperature, and O2 sensor switching characteristics compared to all-cylinder operation. If deactivation is stuck active (or a repair leaves it in an abnormal state), the PCM may not see the operating conditions it expects and will hold the catalyst or fuel-system monitor incomplete. Confirming normal cylinder-count operation is part of correctly wrapping up the repair, not an afterthought.

Q25 Medium Performing Repairs

Many modern vehicles use an engine Auto Stop-Start (ASS) system. How can this affect completing OBD readiness monitors during a post-repair verification drive?

Why

Monitors like catalyst efficiency and EVAP often need sustained engine-run conditions to complete a full test. A stop-start system that's constantly killing and restarting the engine in traffic can break up that continuity. Many shops handle this by using the OEM's documented way to temporarily disable ASS (where one exists) for the verification drive, then re-enabling it afterward — never by disconnecting or permanently defeating the system.

Q26 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

A wideband air-fuel (A/F) ratio sensor, used as the upstream sensor on most 2016-and-newer vehicles, differs from a traditional narrow-band O2 sensor in that it:

Why

A narrow-band O2 sensor essentially reports a digital-feeling rich/lean flip around stoichiometric. A wideband A/F sensor instead produces a signal proportional to the actual air-fuel ratio across a much broader range, giving the PCM finer, faster resolution for fuel control — which is why most manufacturers moved the upstream sensor to this type, even though the downstream position generally still uses a conventional switching O2 sensor for catalyst monitoring.

Q27 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

What is the purpose of a secondary air injection (AIR) system in a cold-start emissions strategy?

Why

A cold catalyst does almost nothing until it reaches light-off temperature, and a cold engine is at its dirtiest right after startup. Secondary air injection pumps extra fresh air into the hot exhaust stream during that warm-up window, which promotes an exothermic oxidation reaction that both cleans up raw HC/CO and helps heat the catalyst faster — a failed AIR pump or blocked check valve is a common source of cold-start-only emissions codes.

Q28 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

Comparing a P0442 (EVAP small leak) to P0455/P0457 (EVAP large leak/no flow), the key difference is:

Why

The EVAP monitor's pressure/vacuum decay test is calibrated to detect leaks of specific approximate sizes. A small pinhole or a cracked hose might only be big enough to trip the small-leak threshold (P0442), while anything that breaks the seal more dramatically — most commonly a loose or missing gas cap — fails the much easier-to-trip large-leak threshold (P0455, sometimes paired with P0457 specifically flagging the cap).

moving dots = fuel vapor flow   dashed = electrical control/sense line. A small leak (~0.020") sets P0442; a large leak (~0.040", e.g. a loose gas cap) sets P0455/P0457.

Q29 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

Why does a loose or missing gas cap commonly fail the EVAP portion of a Smog Check on 2000-and-newer vehicles?

Why

The EVAP monitor treats the fuel tank, lines, canister, and cap as one sealed pressure vessel and checks whether it holds a small commanded pressure or vacuum for a set time. Since the cap is one seal in that system, even a cap that's simply not clicked fully closed can prevent the system from holding, and the PCM has no way to distinguish that from a genuine large leak elsewhere in the system.

Q30 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

What must generally be true about fuel level for the PCM to actually execute (not just have previously completed) an EVAP monitor on many vehicles?

Why

A nearly full or nearly empty tank distorts the vapor space volume the EVAP monitor's math depends on, so most manufacturers gate the monitor to only run within a mid-range fuel level window. The exact percentages vary a little by manufacturer, but the general “not too full, not too empty” rule of thumb is worth knowing when a customer says their EVAP monitor “won't ever complete” and they always fill up right before checking.

Q31 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

A high-pressure direct-injection fuel pump on a GDI engine that loses pressure at higher RPM/load would most likely first present as which type of code or symptom?

Why

GDI systems depend on a mechanically driven high-pressure pump maintaining rail pressure well above what a low-pressure port-injection pump ever needs. A pump that's marginal will keep up at light load/idle but fall behind as injector demand climbs with RPM and load, starving cylinders of fuel exactly when they need the most — showing up as load-dependent lean trim and/or misfire rather than an idle-only complaint.

Q32 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

A P0300 random/multiple-cylinder misfire code, if left unrepaired and the vehicle continues to be driven, poses the greatest risk of causing which secondary failure relevant to a Smog Check?

Why

A misfiring cylinder sends raw, unburned fuel into the exhaust instead of combusting it in the chamber. That fuel then ignites and burns inside the hot catalytic converter, which can melt the substrate or cook the washcoat well beyond its design temperature. This is exactly why diagnostic sequencing matters (see Question 50): fixing the misfire first often resolves what looked like a separate catalyst problem.

Q33 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

On a hybrid vehicle where the gasoline engine only runs intermittently, what practical challenge does this create for completing OBD readiness monitors after a repair?

Why

Every engine-dependent monitor needs the engine actually running under the right conditions to evaluate. A hybrid's control strategy may keep the gas engine off for long stretches in city driving, so simply putting miles on the odometer doesn't guarantee those monitors get their chance to run. Many manufacturers publish (or techs discover through experience) a specific drive pattern — often involving sustained highway speed — that forces enough continuous engine-on time to complete them.

Q34 Medium Diagnosing Test Failures

A contaminated/dirty Mass Air Flow (MAF) sensor commonly causes which combined symptom pattern?

Why

A film of oil, dust, or silicone contamination on the MAF's hot-wire or hot-film element insulates it slightly, so it reads less airflow than is actually passing. Since the PCM meters fuel based on that (under-reported) airflow signal, the mixture goes lean, and the effect scales with how much air is actually moving — worse at higher RPM/load, sometimes barely noticeable at idle, which can make this fault look like a completely different problem if you only check idle data.

Q35 Medium Consumer Obligations / Program Rules

A vehicle's MIL is off and there are no confirmed (stored) codes, but three OBD monitors show “not ready.” Under the current BAR rule described in Question 16, what does this mean for the OBD inspection?

Why

Monitor readiness and MIL/DTC status are two separate pass/fail criteria within the OIS test, not one combined check. A perfectly clean-running car with no codes can still fail purely on unset monitors — commonly caused by a recent battery disconnect, a repair that reset monitors, or simply not enough driving since the last reset — which is precisely why the October 2025 tightening of the not-ready allowance (Question 16) matters in real shop practice.

Hard — Complex, Multi-System Scenarios Q36–50

Q36 Hard Diagnosing Test Failures

Using enhanced (Mode $06) scan data, a technician can view:

Why

Mode $06 exposes the PCM's internal pass/fail math for monitors that don't run continuously (catalyst, EVAP, EGR, etc.) as raw numeric test results against their calibrated limits. A component that's trending toward its limit but hasn't crossed it yet won't show as a code, but it will show up in Mode $06 as a value creeping toward the threshold — genuinely useful for catching a marginal part before it fails a Smog Check outright, and for confirming a repair actually improved the margin rather than barely scraping by.

Q37 Hard Diagnosing Test Failures

The EVAP monitor consistently shows “not ready” after every attempted drive cycle on a 2020 vehicle, despite no stored codes and no leaks found on a smoke test. The technician notes fuel level has been at 95% every time they've checked. What is the most likely explanation?

Why

This ties directly back to Question 30: a clean smoke test and no stored codes point away from an actual leak, and a consistently near-full tank across every attempt is the tell. The fix here isn't a parts swap at all — it's simply driving the vehicle down into the monitor's required fuel-level window (or advising the customer not to top off right before the recheck) so the test finally gets a chance to run.

Q38 Hard Diagnosing Test Failures

A 2021 vehicle throws intermittent U0100 (Lost Communication with ECM/PCM) and U0121 (Lost Communication with ABS Module) codes, and several unrelated monitors are stuck “not ready.” What does this pattern suggest about the underlying fault?

Why

U-codes (network communication codes) affecting multiple unrelated modules at once, combined with stalled monitors across systems that shouldn't otherwise be connected, is a strong signature of a shared-bus problem rather than a component failure. On a modern CAN-based architecture, one bad connector, a chafed harness section, or a marginal module can intermittently disrupt network traffic that several other modules (and the PCM's ability to gather monitor data) depend on.

Q39 Hard Performing Repairs

Diagnosing a P0430 (Catalyst Efficiency, Bank 2), a technician finds the Bank 2 downstream O2 sensor waveform nearly matches a known-good Bank 1 downstream trace, and a smoke test reveals an exhaust leak at the Bank 2 downstream O2 sensor bung caused by a loosely seated sensor. What should the technician do first?

Why

A leak right at the downstream sensor bung draws in outside air at exactly the measurement point the catalyst monitor depends on, which can make a perfectly good converter look inefficient by skewing the sensor's reading. The correct repair sequence is to fix the actual verified problem (the leak) first, clear the code, and let the monitor re-run on a sealed system before spending the customer's money on a converter that may not have been the real issue at all.

Q40 Hard Diagnosing Test Failures

What does “two-trip” versus “one-trip” fault-detection logic determine in OBD-II diagnostics?

Why

Most faults get a second chance to confirm themselves before the MIL comes on, which avoids nuisance lights from a one-time glitch. But certain faults — most notably a misfire severe enough to risk cooking the catalytic converter — are treated as one-trip faults (and can even command a blinking MIL) because waiting for a second confirming drive cycle would risk real damage in the meantime.

Q41 Hard Performing Repairs

After replacing a catalytic converter to resolve a P0420, which verification step is essential before returning the vehicle to the customer or presenting it for a Smog Check re-test?

Why

Clearing a code only proves the code is gone at that instant — it says nothing about whether the repair actually fixed the underlying problem. Running the monitor to a completed, passing result is the only real confirmation, and it's also a practical necessity: showing up for a Smog Check re-test with the relevant monitor still “not ready” from a fresh code-clear will simply fail the vehicle again on readiness grounds.

Q42 Hard Performing Repairs

A vehicle fails Smog Check for “monitors not ready” shortly after a technician disconnected the battery to replace an unrelated part (e.g., an alternator). What is the correct professional approach?

Why

Disconnecting the battery clears learned PCM data, including monitor status, on essentially every vehicle. This is a routine, well-understood consequence of battery work — not a defect — and the honest, correct fix is simply driving the vehicle enough, with enough variety in conditions, to let the monitors re-run and report their results, then re-testing once they do.

Q43 Hard Consumer Obligations / Program Rules

Under BAR's consumer-protection requirements, what is a licensed technician/station generally required to do regarding an inspection or repair estimate?

Why

California's Automotive Repair Act and BAR regulations exist specifically to prevent surprise bills and steer eligible consumers toward the financial help available to them. A tech who fails a vehicle without disclosing the estimated repair cost up front, or who never mentions CAP/repair cost waiver eligibility to a consumer who may qualify, is falling short of this consumer-facing obligation — a substantial share of the real exam's content, since it's the area many hands-on techs are least practiced at.

Q44 Hard Consumer Obligations / Program Rules

Under the Consumer Assistance Program (CAP), what forms of assistance may an eligible low-income vehicle owner qualify for after their vehicle fails a Smog Check?

Why

CAP offers two main paths for income-qualified owners of a failing vehicle: a capped repair-assistance subsidy to help pay for the emissions repairs needed to pass, or a retirement payment if the owner instead chooses to scrap the vehicle. Both the income threshold and the dollar caps are set by BAR and adjusted periodically, so a technician advising a customer should confirm the current figures directly at bar.ca.gov rather than quoting a number from an older source.

Q45 Hard Consumer Obligations / Program Rules

For a Referee-approved engine change, what must the replacement engine and its emissions equipment generally satisfy?

Why

The engine-change rule exists to prevent a newer, cleaner vehicle from being fitted with an older, dirtier engine and its correspondingly weaker emissions controls. The Referee verifies the donor engine's certification family, model year, and weight class are appropriate for the recipient vehicle, and that all of the donor's original emissions hardware (not just the long block) came along with it.

Q46 Hard Diagnosing Test Failures

A 2019 turbocharged vehicle has an intermittent P0299 (turbo/supercharger underboost) and fuel trims trending positive (lean) primarily under boost, with normal trims at idle and light cruise. What does this pattern most strongly suggest?

Why

The key detail is that this only shows up under boost. Air lost after the MAF but before the throttle body/intake manifold has already been metered by the PCM, so under boost the engine actually receives less air-and-fuel than commanded (since the PCM thinks the escaped air made it into the cylinders), producing a load-dependent lean condition alongside the underboost code itself — different from a leak upstream of the MAF, which tends to show up worst at idle instead (compare Question 19).

Q47 Hard Diagnosing Test Failures

After a full battery disconnect, which monitors typically require the LEAST additional drive time to complete, and which typically require the MOST?

Why

Continuous or near-continuous monitors (misfire, fuel system, O2 heater) generally just need normal driving for a short while. Monitors like EVAP (which may require a cold-soak start, a specific fuel level window, and a quiet-engine period) and catalyst (which needs sustained steady-state operation to get a stable comparison between upstream/downstream sensors) are far pickier about conditions, which is exactly why a customer who “drove it around the block” after a repair often comes back with those two specific monitors still not ready.

Q48 Hard Diagnosing Test Failures

On a 2015-and-newer light-duty diesel pickup equipped with OBD, which of the following would most likely be the basis for a Smog Check failure, rather than a traditional opacity (smoke) failure?

Why

Once a diesel is OBD-equipped, California's inspection leans on the same OBD Inspection System logic used for gasoline vehicles — MIL status, stored codes, and readiness — rather than a standalone smoke-opacity measurement. That means a DPF fault (clogged filter, failed differential-pressure sensor, incomplete regeneration) or an SCR/NOx aftertreatment fault commanding the MIL is now the more relevant failure mode for a technician to understand than a visual smoke check.

Q49 Hard Consumer Obligations / Program Rules

A vehicle is flagged as a “gross polluter” (tailpipe emissions far exceeding the applicable standard). Which statement about it is TRUE?

Why

This combines two threads from earlier in the exam: the STAR/Referee certification restriction on higher-risk vehicles (Question 1) and the CAP financial-assistance program (Question 44). A gross polluter designation routes the vehicle toward the stations with tighter oversight, while simultaneously opening the door to help paying for the needed repairs (or retiring the vehicle) if the owner qualifies by income.

Q50 Hard Performing Repairs

A 2022 vehicle fails its Smog Check with the MIL commanded on and stores P0301 (Cylinder 1 Misfire) and P0420 (Catalyst Efficiency, Bank 1). Diagnosis confirms a failed ignition coil on cylinder 1 causing the misfire; a borescope and smoke test show the catalyst substrate itself is undamaged with no exhaust leaks. What is the correct repair order and reasoning?

Why

This closes the loop on the misfire-damages-catalyst relationship from Question 32. Because the borescope/smoke test already confirmed the converter substrate is physically intact, the far more likely explanation is that raw fuel from the misfire was disrupting the catalyst's oxygen-storage behavior enough to trip the efficiency monitor — without necessarily having done permanent damage. Fixing the actual root cause first, then re-testing before ever replacing a physically sound converter, is both the technically correct sequence and the one that best serves the customer's wallet.
